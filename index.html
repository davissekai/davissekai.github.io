01 / about

I build things that hold up.

I'm a graduate student in Climate Change and Sustainable Development (MPhil) at the Centre for Climate Change and Sustainability Studies, University of Ghana, and an Industrial Engineering graduate of KNUST. Before that, I did national service with the Africa Climate Collaborative at UG, supporting climate-smart entrepreneurs and innovation hubs across the country.

Today I build AI-assisted tools that read satellite imagery and everyday evidence, like a phone video of a muddy river, and say only what the data can support. I care as much about how a system says what it knows as about what it detects: badges instead of fake-precise scores, claims that stop where the evidence stops, and checks frozen before the test.

Alongside that, I research Ghana's emerging carbon market: how MRV and carbon governance can be credible internationally, workable locally, and fair to the people and businesses closest to the land.

Currently building Two Eyes and learning forest MRV in public.

02 / selected_work

Things I've built.

01

Two Eyes

Citizens film a muddy river from a bridge; Two Eyes traces it upstream, finds new forest clearings in Sentinel-2 imagery, cross-checks them against radar alerts and forest-loss datasets, and returns a dated, anonymous evidence card for WhatsApp. Validated against a measured galamsey clearing in Atewa before launch. Built with SAM 3.1 and Muse Spark for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon, 2026.
